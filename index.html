Detection vs. Prediction: Decoupled Machine Learning Architecture

A critical design decision in this engineering system is the decoupling of spatial perception from temporal forecasting into two specialized models. The detection model (YOLOv8) operates strictly on spatial single-frame representations to extract discrete vehicle bounding boxes, bounding coordinates, and classification confidence scores at 5–10 FPS per camera.

In contrast, the prediction engine (LSTM) processes historical sequences of aggregated passenger-car-unit (PCU) densities across 30-minute sliding windows. Attempting to unify these tasks into a single monolithic end-to-end model causes severe computational latency, catastrophic forgetting during domain shifts, and prevents deterministic rule validation required by municipal transportation safety protocols.

Why Ambulance Detection is the Most Challenging Class

Ambulance classification represents the most complex perceptual objective in urban traffic computer vision. Standard benchmark corpora (such as MS COCO) do not possess a discrete "ambulance" class, requiring specialized transfer learning. Visually, ambulances share identical chassis silhouettes with delivery vans, utility trucks, and passenger shuttles, differing solely in livery markers (red crescents, caducei) and rooftop flashing beacon lightbars.

During severe occlusions in dense bumper-to-bumper jams or low-light night conditions where retroreflective strobe reflections bleed into camera sensors, purely vision-based detectors exhibit borderline confidence. To mitigate this critical safety failure mode, our architecture incorporates a two-stage classifier with high-resolution regional zoom verification combined with an acoustic siren frequency spectrogram CNN fusion pipeline.

Priority 1 (Highest)
Emergency Override		 Ambulance detected in any lane (Visual conf ≥ 0.85 OR Audio confirmation) Instantly terminate active phase with 3s amber clearance; force detected lane GREEN; lock all opposing lanes to RED; dispatch corridor pre-emption message to downstream junction via MQTT.
Priority 2
Predictive Congestion		 LSTM forecasts density increase > 20% in next 90s (Queue accumulation shock) Extend current green allocation preemptively or re-order upcoming phase schedule to flush approaching bottleneck before standing queue spills past intersection approach boundaries.
Priority 3
Current Density		 Real-time density comparison across lanes (PCU weighted vehicle count) Allocate green phase time dynamically and proportionally: higher real-time queue density receives longer phase split according to formula: T_green = base + k × PCU.
Priority 4 (Fallback)
Fairness / Starvation		 Lane red duration exceeds maximum threshold (> 120s) Force immediate phase switch to starved approach regardless of low comparative density, guaranteeing upper-bound wait time enforcement for all drivers.

Feature Modules

Dynamic Green-Time Calculation
Computes adaptive phase duration based on real-time vehicle accumulation, preventing unnecessary idle red lights on congested approaches. Phase duration is bounded within safety bounds.
Formula:
green_time = base_time + k × vehicle_count
Where base_time = 15s, k = 1.2, clamped [15s – 90s].
Worked Example: vehicle_count = 24
green_time = 15 + (1.2 × 24) = 43.8s
Emergency Corridor Clearing
Establishes high-priority green waves across consecutive arterial junctions by broadcasting MQTT pre-emption packets before the ambulance reaches downstream intersections.

Microscopic Traffic Simulation via SUMO (Simulation of Urban Mobility)

To evaluate the efficacy of the proposed dynamic signal control framework against real-world traffic benchmarks without risking roadway disruptions, extensive synthetic experiments were performed inside the SUMO (Simulation of Urban Mobility) microscopic traffic simulator. A high-fidelity model of an asymmetrical 4-lane intersection was constructed, complete with realistic acceleration curves, car-following Krauss dynamics, lane-changing models, and empirical origin-destination matrices.

Over 200 simulation hours spanning peak-hour surges (1,800 vehicles/hour/approach) and off-peak periods, the proposed YOLOv8-LSTM adaptive framework reduced mean intersection vehicle wait times by 27.4% (from 48.2s down to 35.0s per vehicle) and cut 95th-percentile queue lengths by 34.1% compared to a conventional fixed-time cycle controller. Crucially, in 50 randomized emergency vehicle injection trials, average clearance latency was compressed to 8.2 seconds, eliminating stationary queue blockage for arriving ambulances.

System Scalability & City-Wide Grid Expansion

The modular architecture is inherently designed for horizontal scalability across distributed urban road grids. Because each junction executes inference and priority arbitration locally on dedicated edge hardware, bandwidth requirements to central traffic headquarters are strictly limited to lightweight JSON telemetry payloads (less than 15 KB/s per junction).

Inter-junction coordination is achieved via lightweight publish-subscribe MQTT brokers over municipal fiber or 5G private APNs. When an ambulance priority event occurs at node J, an upstream pre-emption message propagates along the planned navigation corridor, allowing downstream nodes to clear standing queues prior to vehicle arrival without requiring centralized monolithic computing clusters.

Current Engineering Limitations & Future Work

While the system achieves high reliability under standard daylight conditions, several physical and environmental constraints exist. In torrential monsoon rain, lens splash distortion and severe lens glare degrade computer vision mAP by up to 14.8%. Heavy bumper-to-bumper occlusions where smaller two-wheelers are masked behind large commercial trucks occasionally induce queue estimation errors.

Additionally, thermal dissipation and initial capital expenditure for ruggedized IP67 edge AI compute enclosures present budgetary constraints for broad municipal rollouts. Future iterations plan to integrate 77 GHz millimeter-wave radar fusion to preserve high detection accuracy during zero-visibility fog and night conditions.
